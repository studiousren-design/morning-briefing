**NVIDIA (NVDA)** — The dominant story today is regulatory: the **US Department of Justice is investigating Nvidia's licensing deal with AI chip startup Groq**, reported by the New York Times and confirmed by Bloomberg and Axios. The DOJ opened its probe shortly after the deal was announced in December and has sent Nvidia a formal request for information; regulators are examining whether pairing a licensing deal with a talent hire functions as a disguised merger designed to dodge standard antitrust notification. Nvidia has defended the arrangement publicly. A DOJ fine looks more likely than an unwinding of the deal, but this is a genuine overhang worth watching, and it comes on top of the ongoing short-seller scrutiny (Jim Chanos) of Nvidia's AI-chip rental economics. ## SEGMENT 4 — THE BIGGER PICTURE: US MARKET & MACRO

### Yesterday's Session (Thursday, September 10th)

Another down day: the **Dow fell over 300 points (about -0.6%)**, the **S&P 500 lost around 0.6%**, and the **Nasdaq Composite slipped about 0.7%**. Futures were pointing modestly higher heading into today's session (S&P futures +0.1%, Dow futures +0.05%, Nasdaq futures +0.06%), as traders positioned ahead of this morning's inflation data.

### Today's Main Event: The Consumer Price Index

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics releases the **Consumer Price Index for August at 8:30am US Eastern time (1:30pm UK time) today, Friday September 11th**. This is the headline measure of inflation actually felt by consumers, and it's the single most important data point ahead of next week's Federal Reserve meeting. Consensus estimates point to headline inflation of around **3.3–3.4% year-on-year**, roughly in line with July's 3.4% reading — still well above the Fed's 2% target. This print will be scrutinised closely for how much of the recent oil-price surge is beginning to feed through into broader prices.

### The Big Story: Escalating Middle East Conflict and Oil Prices

The US-Iran conflict continues to intensify and now stretches into its **seventh month**. Oil has extended its rally to its **highest levels since May**: crude was trading around **$102–103 a barrel**, with **West Texas Intermediate above $104** and **Brent crude settling near $108** — the highest in nearly four months. Iran's Houthi allies in Yemen have struck multiple Saudi energy facilities, and **Saudi Arabia's crude production fell sharply in August, down about 1.9 million barrels a day to 6.238 million barrels a day** — its lowest level since 1990. Iran has said it is "ready for more intense conflict," and there's little sign of a near-term ceasefire. This remains the single biggest live risk factor for markets, and it's now colliding directly with today's inflation data.
