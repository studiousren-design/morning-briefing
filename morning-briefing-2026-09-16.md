## SEGMENT 4 — THE BIGGER PICTURE: US MARKET & MACRO

### Where Things Stand Heading Into Today

US stocks fell again Tuesday as traders braced for today's Fed decision: the **S&P 500 slipped 0.45%** to 7,585.73, the **Nasdaq fell 0.78%** to 25,981.57, and the **Dow dropped 328 points (0.63%)** to 52,093.11. The **10-year Treasury yield surged to around 5.04%**, its highest level since 2007. Today is the main event.

### Story One: The Federal Reserve Decides Today

The Fed's rate announcement lands at **2:00pm US Eastern (7:00pm UK time) today**, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference at 2:30pm Eastern. The current target range is 3.50%–3.75%, held since December 2025. Market-implied odds of a **quarter-point hike** (to 3.75%–4.00%) vary notably by source — reported anywhere from roughly 56% to as high as 88% depending on the prediction market or tool cited — but the consensus lean is that a hike is more likely than not, which would be the **first Fed rate hike since 2023**. The shift follows Chair Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole speech, hot inflation data, and now the added inflationary pressure from surging oil prices. This is, without question, the single most important event for markets today.

### Story Two: Oil Stays Elevated Despite Saudi Pipeline Repairs

Saudi Arabia's **East–West Crude Oil Pipeline is reportedly back to full capacity (around 7 million barrels/day)** after repairs following last week's drone strikes — but oil prices have stayed elevated rather than easing, with **Brent crossing $108/barrel** and **WTI around $102**, as security concerns persist. At least two tankers have reportedly come under attack near the **Strait of Hormuz** since the weekend, and Goldman Sachs has flagged a scenario where Brent could exceed **$120** if disruptions continue. Global oil inventories are already reported to be roughly 1 billion barrels lower than usual, leaving little cushion for further shocks — a real inflation risk landing on the same day as the Fed's decision.

### Story Three: AI Industry Coordinates on Safety

What began as reports of informal talks has now been confirmed directly: **OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind are formally coordinating on AI-safety measures**, according to OpenAI policy chief Chris Lehane, who says the discussions have been under way for weeks and don't require an antitrust waiver. This follows **Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's essay published Saturday**, calling for the industry to collectively slow the pace of frontier AI development to let risk-prevention catch up — an essay reportedly backed by Sam Altman, Google's Demis Hassabis and Elon Musk's SpaceXAI. Adding tension: a former Anthropic employee has publicly warned the industry is "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence," while OpenAI's chief scientist says no lab has yet solved alignment sufficiently to justify scaling at maximum speed. This remains directly relevant to NVDA, QCOM, NOW and VCX.
