## SEGMENT 4 — THE BIGGER PICTURE: US MARKET & MACRO

### Yesterday's Session (Wednesday, September 9th)

It was another down day: the **Dow Jones fell 405.41 points (-0.77%)** to close at 52,380.66, the **S&P 500 dropped 0.48%** to 7,636.36, and the **Nasdaq Composite fell 0.64%** to 26,253.34. Rising oil prices and persistent Middle East tension were the dominant drag, alongside a jump in Treasury yields — the 10-year yield climbed to **4.857%** after the US Treasury announced it would triple its buyback operation of longer-dated government debt to $6 billion.

### The Big Story: Escalating Middle East Conflict and Oil Prices

The conflict between the United States and Iran continues to intensify. Oil climbed to its **highest level since May**, with reports of a **second, previously undisclosed Iranian attack on US Navy ships** pushing prices higher. **Brent crude rose to around $99 a barrel and US benchmark crude (West Texas Intermediate) to around $94–97 a barrel**, with some reports noting crude briefly touching the $100 mark. Separately, **Iran-backed Houthi forces attacked multiple Saudi Arabian energy facilities** — in the cities of Abha, Khamis Mushait, Jazan and Najran — forcing a temporary halt to some operations. **Goldman Sachs has raised its oil price forecasts**, now projecting Brent and West Texas Intermediate crude at $85 and $80 a barrel respectively by December 2026, and has warned Brent could climb above $120 a barrel in 2027 if Gulf oil output stays roughly 4 million barrels per day below pre-war levels. This remains the single biggest risk factor for markets right now — a genuine, live escalation rather than background noise.

### Today's Main Event: The Producer Price Index

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics releases the **Producer Price Index for August at 8:30am US Eastern time (1:30pm UK time) today** — this is a measure of inflation at the wholesale, producer level, and a key input ahead of the Federal Reserve's rate decision. Consensus estimates point to a **monthly increase of around 0.3–0.4% for the headline figure**, with core prices (excluding food and energy) expected up around 0.2–0.3%. On an annual basis, headline producer price inflation is expected to come in around 5.3%, and core around 4.6% — both up from July. This print, combined with tomorrow's **Consumer Price Index for August** (the measure of inflation actually felt by consumers), is described consistently as the key swing factor heading into next week's Federal Reserve meeting.
