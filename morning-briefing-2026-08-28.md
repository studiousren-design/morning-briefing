## SEGMENT 3 — YOUR PORTFOLIO: STOCK-BY-STOCK RUNDOWN

Big week for your tech and semiconductor names, with Nvidia's earnings dominating the headlines. Here's the full rundown on your tickers:

### Semiconductors & Technology

**NVIDIA (NVDA)** — Nvidia reported fiscal Q2 2027 earnings after the close on Wednesday (August 26th): earnings of **$2.22 per share** — more than double a year ago — on revenue of **$96.2 billion**, up **106%** year-over-year. Guidance for the current quarter came in at **$106–110 billion**, beating estimates, and the CFO said the company expects roughly **70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028**, well above the ~45% analysts had penciled in. The stock jumped nearly **9%** on Thursday, its best day since early August, and of 61 analysts covering it, 58 rate it Buy or Strong Buy with an average price target implying about **44% upside**. However, futures cooled slightly overnight as initial enthusiasm about the "artificial intelligence trade" waned ahead of today's Fed speech.

**Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM)** — Continues ramping production of its advanced 3-nanometer chips amid strong demand. There's also a reported potential joint venture with Sony for image sensors — a strategic growth signal. Note for your calendar: TSM's next **cash dividend has an ex-date of September 16th**.

**Qualcomm (QCOM)** — Traded between **$161.92 and $166.95** on Thursday, closing around **$164.65** on above-average volume. No major company-specific catalyst identified for today; it's trading in sympathy with the broader semiconductor rally.

**Marvell Technology (MRVL)** — Reported Q2 fiscal 2027 earnings after the close Thursday: **94 cents per share** versus 93 cents expected, with Q3 guidance of **$1.05–$1.15** versus a $1.08 consensus — a solid, in-line beat. Despite that, the stock fell sharply in after-hours trading, down about **7.4%** to roughly $223, suggesting the market wanted more given how high the artificial intelligence chip bar has been set this week.

### Big Tech

**Alphabet / Google (GOOG)** — Google Cloud launched **Gemini AI for Finance** this week with Deutsche Bank and CME as partners. Google Cloud revenue surged **82% year-over-year to $24.8 billion** last quarter with margins improving to 36%. There are also reports of a rotation among major investors — some reducing Palantir positions in favor of Alphabet, seen as a more consensus AI winner right now.

**Apple (AAPL)** — No major stock-specific news for today, but a big date is now confirmed: Apple's iPhone event is set for **Wednesday, September 9th**, where the iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, and a new foldable "iPhone Ultra" are expected. It will also be the first Apple keynote with **John Ternus as CEO**, who takes over from Tim Cook on September 1st — a leadership transition worth watching for how markets react.

**Palantir (PLTR)** — Beat on top and bottom lines in early August, and gained after William Blair flagged its Maven program as on track for **$1 billion in annual recurring revenue** on August 26th. That said, shares have been volatile, falling as much as 3.4% earlier in the week, and there are reports of some large investors trimming positions in favor of Alphabet.

### Healthcare & Robotics

**Intuitive Surgical (ISRG)** — Traded between **$367 and $372** on Thursday. The stock is down about **34% year-to-date** despite 19% revenue growth last quarter, weighed down by slowing US procedure growth, particularly bariatric surgery, and hospital capital constraints. On the positive side, it was **upgraded to Outperform by Oppenheimer** on August 17th, and received a **Buy rating from Bank of America** on August 11th, both citing a lack of US competition.

### Gold

**SPDR Gold MiniShares (GLDM)** — Gold's spot price is sitting around **$4,594 an ounce** this morning, near record territory, as safe-haven demand stays firm ahead of today's Fed speech and amid ongoing macro uncertainty.

### Enterprise Software

**ServiceNow (NOW)** — Jumped **9% on Thursday** as investors rotated into large-cap enterprise software names, partly on spillover from Salesforce's strong earnings. Shares are up **29% over the past month** following a July earnings beat, with subscription revenue up 24.5% year-over-year. Both **Bank of America (to $150) and Wells Fargo (to $175)** raised their price targets this month.

### Other Tickers — Limited Data Today

- **QuantumScape (QS)** — No new company-specific news since its Q2 results filing in July; no material update for today.

## SEGMENT 4 — THE BIGGER PICTURE: US MARKET & MACRO

Markets closed higher on Thursday, with the **S&P 500 up 0.7% to 7,730.99**, the **Dow up 0.2% to 53,569.44**, and the **Nasdaq Composite up 1.6% to 26,541.35** — the tech-heavy Nasdaq's best day since August 4th, powered almost entirely by Nvidia's post-earnings surge.

### Today's Big Event: Jackson Hole

All eyes are on **Jackson Hole, Wyoming** this morning. Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh** delivers his **first keynote address as Chair** at 10:00am Eastern Time — that's 3:00pm UK time. Markets are pricing in roughly **one-in-three odds of a rate move**, and this speech could be the deciding signal for the Fed's direction into year-end. Inflation remains stubborn at **3.4%**, above the Fed's 2% target, while long-term Treasury yields have climbed to their **highest level since 2007**. Warsh isn't known for telegraphing policy in advance, so there's real two-way risk here: a hawkish surprise could rally the dollar and pressure stocks, while a dovish tone could send risk assets higher. Most of the market reaction is expected to play out in the **two hours after the speech concludes**, so expect volatility this afternoon UK time.

### Ahead of the Speech
US stock futures were little changed to slightly lower overnight, with **Nasdaq 100 futures down about 0.08%** and **Dow futures up around 0.13%**, as traders held back ahead of Warsh's remarks. There are also reports of tension between the Treasury and the Fed over market intervention, adding another layer of uncertainty to today's session.

### Artificial Intelligence Momentum Continues
Nvidia's blowout earnings are still rippling through markets — Asian tech names extended the US rebound overnight, with Taiwan holding firm and Tokyo IT-services stocks catching a bid, though Korean memory names lagged.
